# Whole-game review and coverage

Use only when the user requests a broad game/slop review. For a local HUD, text or animation task, keep the local scope. This route diagnoses coherence and design hypotheses; it does not authorize new combat, economy or progression systems.

## Scope a representative pass

Record the intended experience, audience, current build and available evidence. Follow an actual core loop from entry through a meaningful action/decision, outcome and return. Where progression matters, sample contrasting early/later situations and explain why they were selected. Do not impose a universal session length, number of choices or content quota. Missing late-game access remains a coverage gap, not a failed check.

For large rosters, card pools or catalogs, group by relevant character/family, role, rarity, state and display context. Choose representatives and exceptions that expose different decisions or presentation risks, reusing samples across domains. Record selection reasons and excluded groups, branches and progression ranges. A few normal samples cannot clear the remainder; expand the sample when a finding suggests a shared cause.

Use only relevant references already linked from SKILL.md. Cover these domains briefly; one line per domain is enough, and genuinely inapplicable domains need no invented test:

| Domain | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Art consistency | Do comparable assets follow established identity rules? |
| Form and continuity | Do the same entities retain intended structure across views/states? |
| Information hierarchy | Can required cues and controls be distinguished in the observed conditions? |
| Action feedback | Do actual inputs and meaningful events receive truthful, appropriately distinct feedback? |
| Rule/expression agreement | Do motion, contact, timing and state match intended rules? |
| Meaningful choice | Do advertised alternatives produce the intended differences in decisions or outcomes? |
| Repetition and progression | Do sampled later situations change the experience in the way the game promises? |
| Narrative/content specificity | Do text, speaker, world state and consequences agree and serve their intended role? |
| Feature purpose | What does each questioned feature contribute to the intended experience? |
| Completion and recovery | Does the observed loop close and return coherently, including relevant interruptions? |

Record `domain | coverage | evidence/sample | finding IDs or limitation`. Coverage is **reviewed**, **partial**, **not reviewed**, or **not applicable (reason)**. Keep it separate from defect/hypothesis/proposal/preserve. Reviewed does not mean passed; no finding is not a whole-game certificate. Do not convert coverage into an overall quality percentage.

## Diagnose choice, repetition and purpose carefully

**Choice:** Compare alternatives in the same relevant situation, including cost, range, timing, targeting, prerequisites, synergies and consequences. Identical base damage or a shared animation does not establish identical behavior. Inspect a contrasting situation when it would distinguish roles. Record observed equivalence only within the sample. Cosmetic choices are legitimate. A promised mechanical difference contradicted by implementation can be a defect; a desire for greater depth without that contradiction is a design hypothesis or proposal.

**Repetition:** Compare what players decide, how they respond, enemy/event combinations, pacing and the effect of rewards across sampled stages. Numeric changes can create new tactics through thresholds; identical input can support mastery, rhythm, relaxation or ritual. Repeated assets alone are not evidence of weak progression. Check claims against the game's intended experience; test boredom or preference with players rather than inferring it from counts.

**Feature purpose:** Trace the questioned feature's entry, actions, output and effect on subsequent play. Consider expression, collection, social play, atmosphere and accessibility as valid purposes alongside mechanical advantage. Inspect whether currencies, menus or notifications cause an observed interruption or contradict a promised benefit. Removing a feature can break dependencies or player goals; propose a bounded trial only within the requested scope.

**Experience checkpoints:** Distinguish correct behavior, readable expression and player response. A technically correct loop may still warrant a taste/identity trial; do not require a broken button before discussing it. Conversely, a reviewer's unfamiliarity does not establish human confusion. Give players concrete tasks and record the actual sample/order if testing is available.

## Close the review within its evidence

Prioritize supported consequences and recurrence using the existing finding format. Explain major uncovered domains and the smallest useful next evidence. Keep design proposals distinct from required repairs. When broader redesign is requested, carry the finding, evidence and preservation conditions to bettermakegame; no companion installation or second audit is required. Never treat a local repair or a favorable AI review as evidence of fun, retention or market readiness.
